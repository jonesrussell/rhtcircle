What this is: a working counterdraft that resolves the blank thresholds, contradictions and concentrated powers in the earlier package. It is written as a complete election law for member review and as an extension of a future member-ratified Sagamok constitution. It does not replace that unfinished constitutional work. Formal enactment would still require community consultation, legal review and the required member vote.

Preamble

Sagamok Anishnawbek possess the inherent right to choose our leaders and govern our own affairs. That authority belongs to the people. It is exercised in accordance with Anishinaabe teachings, including honesty, humility, respect, bravery, wisdom, truth and love.

Leadership is an honourable responsibility and a public trust. Elections must be independent, accessible, transparent and fair. Members must be able to hold leaders accountable between elections without surrendering procedural fairness. This Law therefore protects the right to vote, the right to run, the right to know how decisions are made, and the right to recall a leader through a verified petition and secret vote.

Part 1 - Purpose, authority and interpretation

1. Purpose

1.1

This Law governs the selection, term, accountability, recall and replacement of the Chief and Councillors of Sagamok Anishnawbek.

1.2

This Law must be interpreted to protect member sovereignty, meaningful participation, ballot secrecy, procedural fairness and independent election administration.

2. Constitutional relationship, supremacy and consistency

2.1

This Law is limited to leadership selection and accountability. It is intended to implement, and operate as an extension of, a member-ratified Sagamok constitution without replacing that broader constitutional foundation.

2.2

If Sagamok adopts a constitution and a provision of this Law conflicts with it, the constitution prevails. This Law must be interpreted, wherever reasonably possible, consistently with that constitution.

2.3

Subject to section 2.2, if an election policy, form, Council resolution or administrative direction conflicts with this Law, this Law prevails.

2.4

No body may use a policy, contract or form to add a qualification, penalty or restriction not authorized by this Law.

3. Key definitions

3.1

Commission means the independent Sagamok Election Commission. Elector means a Sagamok member who is at least 18 on voting day. Electoral Officer means the independent person appointed by the Commission. Tribunal means a three-person panel drawn from the Election Tribunal roster. Days means calendar days unless this Law says business days.

Part 2 - Members' democratic rights

4.1

Every elector has an equal right to vote by secret ballot, nominate eligible candidates, run for office, receive election information, observe public election processes, seek accommodation, challenge an election decision and participate in a recall or amendment petition.

4.2

No person may retaliate against a member for voting, declining to vote, supporting a candidate, signing or declining to sign a petition, requesting records, bringing an appeal or participating in lawful public discussion.

4.3

Election information must be offered in plain language and, on request where reasonably available, Anishinaabemowin, large print, audio or another accessible format.

Part 3 - Council composition, term and conduct

5. Composition and term

5.1

Council consists of one Chief and twelve Councillors elected at large by all electors for a four-year term.

5.2

The Councillor receiving the most votes is Deputy Chief. If that person declines, the Councillors choose the Deputy by recorded vote.

5.3

Residence inside or outside Sagamok does not affect a member's right to run or vote.

5.4

The term begins seven days after the Electoral Officer certifies the result and continues until the successor takes office. Before taking office, each elected person publicly affirms that they will follow this Law, disclose conflicts and serve the collective interest of Sagamok members.

6. Meetings and decisions

6.1

Council must meet at least monthly. A majority of sitting Councillors forms quorum, but quorum may never be fewer than six Councillors. The Chief is not counted for quorum.

6.2

Council must seek consensus. If consensus is not reached, the Chief must call a recorded vote. The Chief votes only to break a tie.

6.3

Public agendas must be posted at least five days before a regular meeting. Public minutes, including each motion and each official's vote, must be posted within ten business days, subject only to a narrow and recorded lawful confidentiality exception.

7. Fiduciary duty, conflicts and incompatible roles

7.1

Every elected official owes a fiduciary duty to Sagamok and must act honestly, in good faith, for a proper purpose and in the collective interest of members.

7.2

An official with a real, potential or perceived conflict must disclose it before discussion, leave the meeting, take no part in the decision and have the disclosure and recusal recorded in the public minutes and conflict register.

7.3

An elected official may not simultaneously be an employee, paid contractor, officer or paid director of Sagamok or an entity controlled by Sagamok. An unpaid ex officio oversight seat created by law is permitted, but it must be disclosed and carries no additional pay.

7.4

Council remuneration must be recommended by an independent remuneration panel, published in full and take effect only after the next general election.

8. Elder and youth voices

8.1

The Elder Council and Youth Council may each select one non-voting representative to attend Council meetings, receive the same public materials and advise Council. They are not selected or removed by Council.

Part 4 - Independent election bodies

9. Election Commission

9.1

A five-member Election Commission is responsible for election policy administration, appointment and oversight of the Electoral Officer, the Tribunal roster, accessibility, privacy and public reporting.

9.2

Commissioners are chosen by electors through a secret member vote after a 45-day public call for candidates. The same nomination, voting, accessibility and counting protections in this Law apply with necessary changes. At least two Commissioners must ordinarily reside outside Sagamok.

9.3

Commissioners serve staggered six-year terms. At the first selection, the three candidates receiving the most votes serve six years and the next two serve three years. Later selections fill expiring seats for six years.

9.4

A Commissioner may not be a current elected official, candidate, senior employee, paid political staff member, or director or officer of a Sagamok-controlled entity. Close family of a candidate may not participate in decisions concerning that election.

9.5

A Commissioner may be removed for incapacity, serious misconduct, conflict or material breach of this Law only after notice, an independent Tribunal hearing and written reasons. Electors may also recall a Commissioner using Part 11 with necessary changes.

9.6

The Commission publishes its minutes, appointment decisions, conflicts and annual report. Council must provide the approved budget but may not direct the Commission or reduce its funding during an active electoral process.

10. Electoral Officer

10.1

The Commission appoints a trained and experienced Electoral Officer through an open, documented process at least 150 days before a general election.

10.2

The Officer must be independent of Sagamok political and administrative direction and must disclose all actual, potential and perceived conflicts.

10.3

The Commission may remove the Officer only for incapacity, serious misconduct, conflict, material breach of this Law or failure to perform, by a vote of at least four Commissioners with written reasons. A replacement must be appointed within seven days.

11. Tribunal roster

11.1

The Commission appoints a standing roster of seven qualified and impartial members following a public application process. Appointments, qualifications and conflicts are published.

11.2

Each dispute is decided by three roster members selected at random. A panel member with a conflict must recuse and be replaced by another random selection.

11.3

Council, candidates and administration have no role in choosing a panel for a dispute.

Part 5 - Election calendar and voters list

12. Fixed general election

12.1

A general election is held on the second Saturday of August every fourth year, beginning in 2028. Council has no discretion to postpone it except for an emergency under section 12.3.

12.2

The Commission issues the election calendar 180 days before voting day. If any responsible body fails to act, the Commission must perform or assign the required step.

12.3

An election may be postponed only when voting cannot safely or practically occur because of a declared emergency. The Commission must publish reasons, use the shortest necessary postponement and set a new date no more than 30 days later. A Tribunal may review the decision urgently.

13. Voters list

13.1

The Membership Office provides the Electoral Officer only the information reasonably necessary to determine eligibility and contact electors. It must use a secure transfer method.

13.2

The public voters list contains names only. Band numbers, birth dates, addresses and contact details are never publicly posted.

13.3

An elector may correct an omission or error until the close of voting by providing reliable proof. The Officer must give written reasons for refusing a correction, and the Tribunal must hear an urgent appeal before voting closes where practical.

13.4

A candidate receives contact information only for electors who have expressly consented. The candidate must use it only for that election and destroy it within 30 days after all proceedings end.

Part 6 - Nominations and candidates

14. Eligibility

14.1

A candidate must be a Sagamok member, be at least 18 on nomination day and not be disqualified by a final decision under this Law.

14.2

A person's residence, lawful litigation against Sagamok, employment history, income, political opinion or criticism of Council does not disqualify that person.

14.3

A current senior employee may run after taking leave once candidacy is confirmed and must resign the employment position before taking elected office.

14.4

A person may run for only one office in an election. The Electoral Officer, Commissioners and Tribunal roster members must resign their independent role before seeking elected office.

15. Nomination

15.1

Two electors may nominate a candidate electronically, by mail or in person. The nomination meeting remains open at least four hours and must offer remote participation.

15.2

A nominee has five business days to accept. No candidacy fee is charged.

15.3

If a nomination or acceptance contains a correctable omission, the Officer must promptly notify the person and allow three business days to cure it.

15.4

An eligibility decision must include written reasons. A candidate may appeal within three business days and the Tribunal must decide within five business days.

Part 7 - Campaign fairness and finance

16. Equal opportunity

16.1

The Commission provides each candidate equal space for a profile, equal notice of member forums and equal access to any shared campaign communication service.

16.2

Nation money, staff time, vehicles, facilities, mailing lists, social accounts and confidential information may not be used to support or oppose a candidate, except for equal services offered to every candidate.

17. Contributions and spending

17.1

Only individuals may contribute. A person may contribute no more than $500 in money, goods and services to a candidate. Corporate, organizational and anonymous contributions are prohibited.

17.2

A Chief candidate may spend no more than $10,000 and a Councillor candidate no more than $5,000. The Commission may adjust these limits only once every four years for inflation and must publish the adjustment before nominations open.

17.3

Each candidate files a contribution and expense report within 60 days. The Commission publishes the reports with home addresses and sensitive banking details removed.

18. Protected speech and prohibited conduct

18.1

Members and candidates remain free to criticize, question, campaign, organize, use satire and express political opinions.

18.2

Bribery, coercion, threats, intimidation, ballot tampering, deliberate false information about when or how to vote, deliberate false notice that a candidate withdrew, obstruction of election officials, misuse of confidential member data and retaliation are prohibited.

18.3

No election body may punish a person merely for harsh criticism, disputed opinion or a good-faith factual error.

Part 8 - Accessible voting and counting

19. Voting methods

19.1

Every general election and recall vote must provide election-day voting, at least two advance polls, mail voting, secure electronic voting and mobile or home voting where required as an accommodation.

19.2

Electronic voting must be independently security-tested, auditable without revealing votes, free of advertising and tracking, and designed so no administrator can connect a voter to a ballot.

19.3

A voter may receive assistance from a person of their choice after both make a secrecy declaration. Polling places must be physically accessible.

19.4

No copy of a health card may be requested or retained. Identity must be verified through the least intrusive reliable method available.

20. Ballot integrity

20.1

Candidate agents may observe sealing, voting, receipt of remote ballots, counting and recounting without seeing how any person voted.

20.2

A ballot is counted when voter intention is clear and ballot secrecy is preserved. A technical irregularity does not invalidate a vote unless it creates a real risk of ineligibility, duplication or loss of secrecy.

20.3

The Officer records every rejected ballot and the reason. Candidate agents may make numbered objections for the record.

Part 9 - Results, recounts and ties

21.1

The Officer publishes preliminary results promptly and certified results within four days, including turnout by voting method, each candidate's total, rejected-ballot totals and reasons, without compromising secrecy.

21.2

A recount is automatic when the difference is five votes or fewer or 0.5% of valid ballots, whichever is greater. A candidate may request one additional recount within three days without paying a fee where a specific counting concern is identified.

21.3

If a tie remains after recount, a runoff between the tied candidates is held within 21 days. No election is decided by drawing names or casting a fictional ballot.

Part 10 - Complaints, appeals and judicial review

22. Election complaints

22.1

An elector or candidate may file a complaint within 14 days after results are certified or within 14 days after material facts could reasonably have been discovered, but no later than 45 days after certification unless concealment prevented discovery.

22.2

No filing fee, sworn affidavit or lawyer is required. The Tribunal may require a declaration that the information is true to the person's knowledge.

22.3

The Tribunal must disclose the case record, permit written responses, hold an oral hearing where credibility or serious consequences are at issue, provide accommodation, and apply the balance-of-probabilities standard.

22.4

The Tribunal must give written reasons within 21 days after the record closes. It may correct an administrative decision, order a recount, disqualify a candidate, set aside an affected result, order a new vote or make a proportionate compliance order.

22.5

An election is not set aside for a harmless technical defect. A breach must have affected, or been reasonably likely to affect, the result or the fundamental fairness of the election.

22.6

An internal decision is final within Sagamok but does not remove any right to apply for judicial review. The decision notice must state that external court deadlines may apply.

22.7

An elector may apply to the Tribunal without a fee for an urgent order requiring the Commission, Electoral Officer, Recall Officer or Council to perform a mandatory duty under Parts 4 to 15. The Tribunal may set a deadline, assign the duty to an independent person or make another order necessary to prevent delay from defeating member rights.

Part 11 - Member recall

23. Right to initiate recall

23.1

Electors may recall the Chief or any Councillor through a petition followed by a secret recall vote. Recall is a political loss-of-confidence process. Petitioners do not need to prove misconduct.

23.2

A recall may not begin during the first 12 months or final nine months of a term. Only one recall petition may be attempted against the same official in a term.

24. Petition process

24.1

Ten electors file a notice of intent with the Commission. The Commission appoints an independent Recall Officer within five business days.

24.2

Petitioners have 60 days to collect valid signatures from at least 25% of all electors on the voters list as of the notice date. Paper and secure electronic signatures must be available.

24.3

No person may buy a signature, threaten a person, retaliate, sign for another person or use confidential Nation information to solicit signatures.

24.4

The Recall Officer verifies the petition within 15 business days using published neutral rules. The Officer gives written reasons and reports only aggregate totals. Signatures and choices remain confidential.

24.5

Petitioners or the office-holder may appeal verification within seven days. The Tribunal decides within seven days.

25. Recall vote and effect

25.1

A verified petition automatically causes a secret recall vote within 45 days. Council has no veto or postponement power.

25.2

The question is: "Should [office-holder] be removed from the office of [Chief/Councillor] for the remainder of the current term?"

25.3

Recall succeeds when more than half of valid votes favour removal and at least 25% of all electors participate.

25.4

Removal takes effect when results are certified. The removed person may not run in the by-election for the remainder of that term but may run at the next general election.

25.5

The Commission provides petitioners and the office-holder equal space for one statement and one community forum. Nation resources may be used only for neutral administration and equal information.

Part 12 - Vacancies and by-elections

26.1

An office becomes vacant on death, written resignation, certified recall, loss of membership, a final disqualification order or incapacity lasting more than 120 days confirmed through a fair independent process.

26.2

A by-election is held within 90 days unless fewer than nine months remain in the term and the vacancy does not prevent quorum. The Commission, not Council, sets the by-election calendar.

26.3

The Deputy Chief performs the Chief's duties until a Chief by-election is certified. Acting authority does not include changing election bodies or this Law.

26.4

A sitting official may run in a by-election only after resigning before nominations close.

Part 13 - Transparency, records and privacy

27. Public election record

27.1

The Commission maintains a public record containing the current consolidated Law, prior versions, amendment history, calendars, appointments, contracts and compensation totals, conflicts, notices, candidate disclosures, aggregate election reports and Tribunal reasons with necessary personal redactions.

27.2

Removal or replacement of a public document does not erase the prior version. An accessible archive must remain available.

28. Privacy and custody

28.1

Election bodies may collect only information necessary for a stated election purpose, restrict access, keep access logs, use secure Canadian-hosted systems where electronic services are used, and notify affected persons and the Commission of a breach.

28.2

Ballots are separated from identity records as soon as eligibility is confirmed. No record may reveal how a person voted or whether a person signed a recall petition.

28.3

Ballots and case records are retained until every appeal and judicial review is complete plus 90 days, then destroyed in the presence of two independent witnesses. A destruction certificate is published. Aggregate reports and final reasons are retained permanently.

28.4

The Commission remains the records custodian after a temporary Electoral Officer or Tribunal panel finishes its work.

Part 14 - Member petitions, amendment and ratification

29. Ways to propose an amendment

29.1

An amendment may be proposed by Council resolution, Commission resolution or a petition signed by at least 15% of electors.

29.2

A verified member petition is binding on the process. The proposal must proceed to consultation and a member vote within 180 days. Council may recommend for or against it but may not veto, abandon or materially rewrite it.

30. Required review process

30.1

The complete proposed wording, plain-language summary, reasons and comparison with the current Law must be available for at least 45 days.

30.2

At least two community sessions are required, including one hybrid session accessible to members outside Sagamok. Written comments remain open for 30 days.

30.3

An independent working group publishes a response table showing each material proposal, whether it was accepted and why. Legal review may correct legality and clarity but may not change the policy choice without renewed notice.

31. Voting thresholds

31.1

Initial adoption of this community election law requires a majority of valid votes cast in a secret vote in which a majority of all electors participate, together with any additional requirement imposed by applicable law.

31.2

An amendment to a protected provision requires a majority of valid votes cast with participation by at least 40% of electors. Protected provisions are member rights, composition and term, candidate eligibility, independent election bodies, recall, voting methods, disputes, privacy, amendment thresholds and publication requirements.

31.3

Any other substantive amendment requires a majority of valid votes cast with participation by at least 25% of electors.

31.4

Council may not amend this Law by resolution. The Commission may update forms or correct an obvious typographical error only when meaning and rights do not change, after 30 days' public notice.

31.5

No election rule may be changed after an election, recall petition, referendum or appeal begins until that process is complete.

32. Publication and effective date

32.1

Certified results must be published within seven days. Initial adoption also requires Council to provide the Minister with the resolution, proposed code and ratification record required by section 42 of the First Nations Elections Act.

32.2

This Law comes into force only on the effective date of the ministerial order removing Sagamok from the First Nations Elections Act. The complete consolidated Law, vote report, order and effective date must be published in an enduring official location accessible to members.

32.3

After this Law is in force, an amendment takes effect only after the certified result, complete consolidated text and effective date are published.

Part 15 - Transition and review

33.1

The first Commission is selected on the same ballot as this Law or through a separate member vote within 60 days after ratification. Its authority begins only when this Law comes into force. Until then, a neutral transition officer may perform only necessary administrative acts.

33.2

The Chief and Councillors holding office when this Law comes into force continue for the balance of their existing terms, but are immediately subject to this Law, including Part 11 recall. The ratification materials must identify and explain any legal issue affecting this transition before voting begins.

33.3

An elected official holding an incompatible paid position has 90 days after this Law takes effect to leave that position. No additional severance, bonus or penalty may be created because of this requirement.

33.4

The Commission conducts an independent public review after each general election. The review must examine participation, accessibility, complaints, privacy, costs and whether the Law is meeting its Anishinaabe and democratic purposes.

33.5

If one provision is found invalid, the remaining provisions continue to operate to the greatest extent possible.

Reviewer notes: the biggest deliberate changes
  • The law is framed as an extension of a future member-ratified Sagamok constitution, which would prevail over conflicting election-law wording.
  • Recall is based on democratic loss of confidence, not a misconduct trial.
  • Election administration and appeals are removed from Council control.
  • Residency, active litigation and blanket criminal-record restrictions are removed.
  • Election speech is protected while bribery, threats, false voting instructions and misuse of member data remain prohibited.
  • Elected officials cannot hold paid administrative or enterprise positions at the same time.
  • Member petitions create mandatory timelines and cannot be buried by Council.
  • Every substantive amendment requires a member vote. There is no Council-only regulation route.
  • The draft uses participation thresholds to prevent a small turnout from changing protected rights.
